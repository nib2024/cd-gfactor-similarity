# Supplementary Methods

## Similarity and mirror-image analysis of CD g-factor spectra

Each sample set contained nine g-factor spectra ($g=\Delta\varepsilon/\varepsilon$), which were compared pairwise; only the 300–500 nm region was used. Each spectrum was linearly interpolated onto a 0.5 nm grid (280–520 nm) and smoothed with a Savitzky–Golay filter (11-point window, 5.5 nm). Three representations were used: the smoothed spectrum, a high-pass form obtained by subtracting a slowly varying Savitzky–Golay baseline (61-point window, approximately 30 nm), and the first derivative (21-point window, 10.5 nm). The latter two suppress the background common to all samples and emphasize the positions and signs of the Cotton bands. The comparison was designed to distinguish mirror-image spectra, consistent with a reversal of the chiral sense, from non-mirror changes such as a red or blue shift.

For two spectra $A(\lambda)$ and $B(\lambda)$, the Pearson correlation $r(\delta)$ was calculated between $A(\lambda)$ and $B(\lambda+\delta)$ over 300–500 nm, with a rigid shift $\delta$ from −20 to +20 nm in 0.5 nm steps. The same-sense similarity was $S=\max_\delta r(\delta)$, with optimal shift $\delta_S=\arg\max_\delta r(\delta)$. Because a mirror-image CD spectrum has $g\to-g$, the mirror similarity was $M=\max_\delta[-r(\delta)]=-\min_\delta r(\delta)$, with optimal shift $\delta_M=\arg\min_\delta r(\delta)$. The margin $M-S$, or the normalized mirror index $(M-S)/(M+S)$, was used to decide whether the mirror hypothesis was better than the same-sense one. Cosine similarity and the normalized root-mean-square error were calculated as consistency checks.

As independent evidence, Cotton bands were detected on the smoothed spectra as local maxima and minima with a prominence of at least 3× the local high-frequency noise and a width at half prominence of at least 4 nm. Bands from $A$ and $B$ were matched one-to-one within ±20 nm and compared by sign; the amplitude-weighted fraction of matched bands with opposite signs was used to support mirror assignments.

The significance of $S$ and $M$ was estimated from 2000 phase-randomized surrogates of $B$. Phase randomization preserves the power spectrum, and hence the smoothness, of the spectrum while destroying its alignment with $A$. Empirical p-values were obtained from the surrogate distributions and adjusted with the Benjamini–Hochberg procedure within each sample set (36 pairs, q < 0.05). A pair was classified as same-sense when $S\ge0.70$ and $S-M\ge0.20$ (high similarity if $|\delta_S|<3$ nm, shifted otherwise), as mirror when $M\ge0.70$, $M-S\ge0.20$ and q < 0.05 (with band-sign concordance ≥0.60 when at least two bands were matched), and as weak or dissimilar when these criteria were not met. Classification used the derivative representation, with the raw and high-pass results serving as consistency checks. Shifts reaching ±20 nm were reported as "≥20 nm or no resolvable shift".
